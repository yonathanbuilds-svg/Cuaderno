# A useful question beats another promise
Yonathan Zeitoune Mattout | Team 6 | ADVERSARY | Week 15 - Distribution

## 1. My angle
The first audience should be a Spanish-speaking household buyer in CDMX/Estado de Mexico with a furniture quote and a deposit decision approaching, while delivery or assembly remains unclear. Geography is a recruitment hypothesis, not a validated segment. User and potential payer are the same buyer. The free review stays free; MXN 49/project is a proposed later comparison product, not active billing. My adversarial question is whether our content helps this person resolve one missing cost better than a calculator and a message to the store. Reach alone cannot answer it.

## 2. The technology, honestly
Compra con Calma uses deterministic arithmetic. A user-entered known charge is a declaration, not external confirmation. The new guide demonstrates three pieces and links into the existing reviewer; the B1 example opens with a fictional MXN 14,600 base and MXN 700 delivery, leaving assembly and other costs unknown. Its MXN 15,300 is a known subtotal. No AI finds fees, certifies affordability or predicts regret. No personal quote uploads, analytics, accounts, billing or automated outreach are implemented. The three-year evidence-record direction remains conditional on real utility, not technical possibility.

## 3. The world's best attempt
YNAB makes a method recognizable through product demonstrations and visible subscription/trial terms. Its official pricing FAQ states USD pricing and limits direct bank import to selected US, Canadian, UK and EU banks. The Mexican translation is a Spanish, MXN, purchase-specific demonstration with no implied bank integration. We cannot inherit YNAB's outcomes or assume recurring value from one occasional furniture decision. [1]

Splitwise puts convenience such as receipt scanning and currency conversion in Pro. It illustrates a visible free/paid boundary, not proof that this audience will pay. For our context, missing-cost warnings and the question checklist must remain free; paid convenience cannot make an incomplete quote look safe. [2]

IKEA Mexico already publishes delivery and assembly terms and says the final relevant charges are calculated during checkout. Its scope and restrictions matter. Our cross-retailer tool should preserve seller-specific questions, rather than invent a universal delivery fee or position itself as more authoritative than the seller's offer. [3]

## 4. The vacuum I would attack
The proposed vacuum is the gap between an attractive advertised price and the buyer knowing what still needs confirmation before paying a deposit. The Charter has one second-hand case; it does not establish segment size or conversion. The current workaround is assumed to be a quote, notes, a calculator and follow-up messages. PROFECO's furniture guidance recommends asking about freight and installation and requesting written detail. This supports the relevance of the questions, not measured demand for our app. [4]

Our bounded promise: separate entered known amounts from pending charges and give the buyer specific questions. We will not promise guaranteed savings, verified stores, a final delivered total or a 10:1 advantage. We are not targeting people without a current quote, professional procurement teams, loan seekers or shoppers needing product recommendations. A small opt-in test should show A1 and B1 to up to five eligible buyers and observe whether at least three identify the missing charge and next action without explanation. This is a proposed directional threshold; no test result is claimed.

## 5. The shadow
The harm is turning an elegant campaign into false certainty or pressure to buy. Design decisions: numbers are labeled fictitious and subtotal; unknown charges remain unknown; confirmed included services require an explicit 0; safety questions stay free; the CTA leads to inspection rather than checkout. No seller commissions, urgency, automated messages or personal-data capture. The inherited Charter's buyer-control principle constrains distribution as much as calculation.

## 6. My opening position for Team Bending
Proposed assumption to challenge: broader money-saving content will be a better launch than a narrow quote example. The adversarial counterargument is that broad claims outrun our evidence and hide the next useful action. The proposed position is to start at the pre-deposit moment with a demonstrable unknown-versus-zero example. I would revise it if real viewers cannot understand or use the next step, or if another segment demonstrates repeat need. Peer agreement, likes and synthetic persona reactions would not count as that evidence. Before the founder explains, peers should name the purpose, audience, next click and distrust signal; the real mismatch and response belong in the Team Blueprint.
