# Unknown is not a business model
Yonathan Zeitoune Mattout | Team 6 | ADVERSARY | Week 14 - Pricing

## 1. My angle
My job is to kill a price that sounds credible but buys no distinctive benefit. Compra con Calma separates known and unknown furniture-quote charges. A household buyer currently has a free substitute: a calculator, notes and a list of questions for the store. A proposed MXN49 purchase project or MXN149 professional month must beat that substitute for the same task. I oppose the claim that low prices alone kill ventures: raising the price cannot repair an unnecessary product. A refusal to pay is a finding, not a customer defect.

## 2. The technology, honestly
The present tool performs deterministic arithmetic, identifies pending charges, projects revenue from editable assumptions, and saves numeric scenarios on the current device. It neither verifies merchants nor discovers fees. Marking a charge known means that the user supplied it, not that the seller confirmed it externally. There is no active billing or paid-feature entitlement. Those limits matter economically: a calculation that users can reproduce for free creates little defensible scarcity. The value hypothesis must be reusable comparison and evidence organization, not an invented AI advantage.

## 3. The world's best attempts: import the discipline, not the claims
YNAB (US/global) makes the user the payer and states its subscription price. Its official FAQ also states that prices are in USD and direct bank import supports selected US, Canadian, UK and EU banks. It already reaches international users; the Mexico gap is not total absence. The translation is a Spanish, MXN, purchase-specific workflow without assuming Mexican bank coverage. A recurring finance subscription does not prove that an occasional sofa buyer will subscribe. [1]

Splitwise (global) puts paid convenience such as receipt scanning and conversion above a free expense-sharing core. The useful pattern is a deliberate free/paid boundary. It is not absent from Mexico either: localization means matching a purchase project to payment frequency and maintaining essential uncertainty warnings for free. Copying usage limits that interrupt a buyer's safety check would undermine our purpose. [2]

IKEA Mexico already explains its own delivery and assembly services and makes charges depend on the relevant order and service. This is a strong incumbent substitute for an IKEA-only purchase: the supplier is closer to its own quote. Our cross-retailer record would need to preserve each seller's scope and confirmation. We cannot turn an illustrative delivery fee into a universal Mexican tariff. The unresolved gap is comparing incomplete quotes from different stores; the existence of the gap does not prove willingness to pay. [3]

## 4. The vacuum I'd attack: compliments versus payment
Choose one vacuum: the distance between a buyer saying the tool is useful and accepting its actual price. My initial test price is MXN49 once per household project, with MXN149/month as a separate professional hypothesis. The user pays; retailer commissions are excluded. Ask a real potential payer what they do today, show the exact proposed scope and price, then ask whether they would pay and why. Record refusal in their words. Do not count a hypothetical yes as revenue, or a teammate's politeness as demand.

The arithmetic is the first attack. Assumed manual review:30 minutes at MXN120/hour, or60. Assumed tool review:5 minutes, or10, plus49. Baseline/product total cost=60/59=1.02:1. For a professional doing20 reviews monthly,1200/(200+149)=3.44:1. These are unmeasured assumptions. At the assumed5-minute tool time, a household10:1 claim would require a manual cost of at least590, equivalent to295 minutes at120/hour. We have not observed that. Hidden fees are not automatically savings: discovering a fee does not eliminate it.

## 5. The shadow: false precision that sells certainty
The harm is using a polished total, a revenue projection or a10:1 slogan to make incomplete evidence look conclusive. Design decisions: unknown charges never become zero; invalid inputs clear old results and disable copying; projected revenue is labeled as assumptions; prices name their billing period; safety warnings stay free; paid proposals never pretend to be active services. No seller-funded nudge may make buying easier than waiting. These are enforceable interface rules, not a promise to be ethical later.

## 6. My opening position for Team Bending
I partially reject the provocation. A timid price can signal weak value, but an unjustified high price can hide weak evidence. Start at49/project because furniture buying is episodic; test149/month only with repeat professionals. I will change my position if an actual payer identifies repeated value and accepts the scope at that price. I will defend the free warning even if it lowers conversion. The team should vote on buyer-paid projects versus professional subscriptions, preserve dissent, and require the same assumptions on /pricing, the simulator and the memo. No claim of consensus is made before the Fusion.
