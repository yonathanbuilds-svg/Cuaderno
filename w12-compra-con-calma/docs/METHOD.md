# De evidencia a acción
MY METHOD v1 | Yonathan Zeitoune Mattout | Week 12

## When I face a future, I:
1. Identify the person and the concrete decision where the process fails. Evidence: Week 2 merchant proof, Week 7 commuter uncertainty.
2. Separate confirmed facts, assumptions and missing evidence. Evidence: Week 2 operating activity is not repayment capacity; Week 8 no indexed match is not safety.
3. Name who can take the next action and what authority they actually have. Evidence: Week 5 referral navigator; Week 11 inspection owner.
4. Turn missing evidence into explicit questions and next actions. Evidence: Week 3 incident packet; Week 10 unconfirmed delivered costs.
5. Cut to one useful, inspectable interaction. Evidence: Week 6 three rehearsal decisions; Week 10 cost check rather than a life optimizer.
6. Leave the decision and correction path with the person. Evidence: Week 4 equal alternative routes; Weeks 9-10 no ranking or purchase veto.
7. Test the result against a simpler baseline and a real user's task before expanding. Evidence: Week 10 checklist comparison and Week 11 response-capacity gates. Repeated as a stated rule; real-world validation remains my weak point.

## My need-gravity
I keep circling people asked to trust a decision when the evidence or accountable next step is missing. The merchant in Week 2, fraud claimant in Week 3, referral case in Week 5, commuter in Week 7, junior worker in Week 9 and buyer in Week 10 show this recurring concern across different futures.

## My edge
My distinctive emphasis in these briefs is connecting evidence to an operational next step and a named responsible person. A claim that no teammate does this is not established by this individual record.

## My blind spot
I find designing the process easier than validating demand and selling it; I confirmed this during the extraction interview.
